A Story

Somewhere Between
Then and Now

There's a quiet shift that happens as you grow older—so subtle you almost miss it. Not in the big, obvious moments or milestones, but in the way the people who once shared the same small world begin to scatter into lives you can no longer fully see.

The same hallways, the same afternoons, the same familiar rhythms— all of it dissolves, not suddenly, but slowly, like something fading without asking to be held onto. And then, without warning, you notice where everyone has arrived.

Some have built homes filled with new voices. Some have crossed oceans and started again in places that don't know their past. Some stayed, and slowly became part of the place that once held all of you together.

None of it feels closer. None of it feels farther. Just different, in ways that don't ask to be measured.

The past doesn't return as longing. It arrives as fragments—soft, unannounced, almost weightless. A place, a face, a moment suspended somewhere between memory and now. And in those moments, there's a feeling that refuses to be named.

Not quite peace. Not quite distance. Something in between—like standing still while everything else continues to unfold in its own direction. As if life was never meant to be followed as a single path, but something that branches endlessly, quietly carrying each of us away from the same beginning.

There's no urgency in it. No sense of loss. Only the awareness that all these lives—once so closely woven— now exist side by side, complete, untouched by comparison.

And somewhere within it, your own life continues— not ahead, not behind— just moving, as it always was meant to.

Aditya Dwivedi
— Somewhere In Our Little World.
